What you can put under a laser depends on two things: what the material gives off when it burns, and what kind of laser you have. This guide covers the common materials, the ones never to laser, and which laser type handles what. Your machine maker's list and your material's safety data sheet come first; where sources here disagree, that is noted.

## Never laser these

These give off dangerous gases or damage the machine. Trotec, a laser maker, says processing them "creates dangerous gases or dust" that can endanger the user or the machine ([Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing)).

| Material | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| PVC, vinyl and faux leather ("pleather") | PVC produces hydrogen chloride gas, which MIT calls "extremely dangerous". ACE Makerspace adds that it damages optics and corrodes the machine. | [MIT EHS](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/laser-cutter-safety/), [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| Anything containing halogens (chlorine, fluorine, bromine, iodine), including PTFE (Teflon) and PVB | Listed as unsuitable by Trotec, which also says flame-retardant materials often contain bromine | [Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing) |
| Leather and artificial leather containing chromium (VI) | Listed as unsuitable by Trotec | [Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing) |
| Carbon fiber | Trotec lists it as unsuitable; ACE says coated carbon fiber "emits noxious fumes" | [Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing), [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| Epoxy and phenolic resins, and fiberglass (glass in epoxy) | Trotec lists the resins as unsuitable; ACE says fiberglass's epoxy gives off fumes and the glass can't be cut | [Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing), [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| ABS | ACE says it "emits cyanide gas and tends to melt" | [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| Polycarbonate (Lexan) | ACE says it "cuts very poorly, discolors, catch[es] fire", because it strongly absorbs the CO2 laser's infrared light | [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| HDPE (milk bottle plastic) | ACE: "It melts. It gets gooey. Don't use it." | [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| Polystyrene foam | ACE calls it "the #1 material that causes laser fires" | [ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/) |
| Beryllium oxide | Listed as unsuitable by Trotec | [Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing) |

If you don't know what a plastic is, don't laser it. Thunder Laser, another laser maker, says PVC, vinyl "and unknown plastics" should not be processed because they may release toxic or corrosive fumes ([Thunder Laser](https://thunderlaser.com/laser-wiki/material-applications/materials-not-for-laser-processing)).

## Common materials

### Wood

- **Solid wood** is on Epilog's list of materials compatible with its CO2 lasers ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)).
- **Plywood** contains glue and "may not laser cut as well as solid wood", according to ACE Makerspace, which limits it to 1/4 inch in its shop ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)).
- **MDF** is okay to use but may char more when cut. ACE lists "anything with formaldehyde" as never to cut, so check what binder your MDF uses ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)).

### Acrylic

Acrylic comes as cast or extruded sheet, and the two behave differently:

| | Cast | Extruded |
|---|---|---|
| Engraving | Bright white (Trotec) or frosty white (Epilog) | Matte gray (Trotec) or clear (Epilog) |
| Cut edge | Clean, burr-free on both sides (Trotec); not flame-polished (Epilog) | Flame-polished (Trotec, Epilog) |
| Cost | Higher (Trotec) | Lower (Trotec) |

Sources: [Trotec](https://www.troteclaser.com/en/learn-support/helpcenter/material-handling-acrylic), [Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/en-uk/how-it-works/applications/laser-cutting-acrylic/).

So cast suits engraving, and extruded suits cut shapes where a glossy edge matters. ACE Makerspace also says cast "is better suited than extruded acrylic for machining" ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)).

Polycarbonate is clear plastic sheet too, and you should not cut it (see above). If a sheet isn't labeled, find out what it is first.

### Leather

Real leather can be lasered; faux leather is often PVC and must not be. ACE Makerspace says leather "is very hard to cut, but can be done. Results vary", limits it to 1/8 inch, and warns to "be careful about how it is tanned" ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)). Trotec lists leather containing chromium (VI) as unsuitable ([Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing)). Ask your supplier how the leather was tanned before you buy it for laser work.

### Paper, card and fabric

Paper and card stock cut "very well on the laser cutter, and also very quickly", and cloth, felt and cotton "all cut well", though not plastic-coated fabric ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)).

### Glass, ceramic and stone

These can be engraved but not cut. ACE Makerspace lists glass, ceramic and stone as etch only, and warns that stone can "explode" ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)). Epilog lists glass, ceramic, marble and tile as compatible with its CO2 lasers ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)).

### Metals

- **Bare metal:** a CO2 laser marks it only when it is coated with a metal marking solution ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)).
- **Anodized and painted metal:** ACE Makerspace lists them as etch only on its CO2 cutter ([ACE Makerspace](https://wiki.acemakerspace.org/laser-cutter-materials/)). Epilog lists anodized aluminum among materials tested on its fiber laser ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)).
- **Marking bare steel, brass and aluminum directly** is fiber laser work. Epilog's fiber list includes stainless steel, brass and aluminum ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)).

Trotec says to take care with manganese, chromium, nickel, cobalt, copper and lead ([Trotec](https://troteclaser.com/en/faqs/unsuitable-materials-laser-processing)).

## Which laser does what

| Laser type | Good for | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Diode (blue light, e.g. 450 nm) | Glowforge's 6-watt diode machines take materials up to 1/4 inch thick ([Glowforge](https://glowforge.com/b/tech-specs/craft)) | Glowforge says its 6-watt diode Spark can't do glass, clear, translucent or white acrylic, or some blue acrylics ([Glowforge](https://glowforge.com/b/tech-specs/craft)) |
| CO2 | Wood, acrylic, leather, paper, fabric; engraves glass, stone and coated metal ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)) | Marks bare metal only with a marking solution ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)) |
| Fiber | Marking metals and some engineered plastics ([Epilog](https://www.epiloglaser.com/how-it-works/laser-material-compatibility/)) | Epilog's fiber list is for marking, not cutting sheet materials |

## Fumes and fire

Whatever the material, lasering it burns material away, "generating significant amounts of heat" ([UW–Madison EHS](https://ehs.wisc.edu/laser-cutter-safety/)).

- **Ventilation:** UW–Madison says laser cutters "require proper ventilation to prevent fires and exposure hazards", and that some materials "can produce toxic and corrosive fumes" ([UW–Madison EHS](https://ehs.wisc.edu/laser-cutter-safety/)).
- **Never leave it running alone:** "Never leave an operating laser cutter unattended" ([MIT EHS](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/laser-cutter-safety/)).
- **Keep an extinguisher nearby:** MIT says one should be mounted on the wall near the cutter ([MIT EHS](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/laser-cutter-safety/)).
- **Use air assist:** MIT calls it "important for preventing fires (and also helps to make cleaner cuts)" ([MIT EHS](https://ehs.mit.edu/workplace-safety-program/laser-cutter-safety/)).

## Before you laser a new material

1. Find out exactly what it is. No label, no laser.
2. Check it against the "never" list above and your machine maker's list.
3. Read the material's safety data sheet.
4. Run a small test with the ventilation on, and watch it the whole time.

If you need a material your machine can't handle, such as metal marking on a CO2 or diode laser, a laser shop listed on this site may be able to do the job.
