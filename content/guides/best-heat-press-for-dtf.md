DTF transfers ask three things of a heat press: a steady temperature, firm pressure that is even across the whole design, and an accurate timer. This guide covers what the transfer makers ask for, which press features deliver it, and a few example machines with the specs their makers publish. Prices were read from the makers' own pages on November 15, 2026 and change often.

## What DTF transfer makers ask for

Start from the settings published by whoever made your transfers. They differ by maker, and one maker's numbers can be wrong for another's film and adhesive.

| Maker | Temperature | Time | Pressure | Peel | Second press |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [DTF Transfers Now](https://dtftransfersnow.com/dtf-pressing-instructions) (100% cotton) | 300–320°F | 10–15 seconds | Firm | Hot or cold | 10 seconds under a Teflon sheet or linen cloth |
| [T-shirt.ca](https://t-shirt.ca/dtf-knowledge-hub/heat-press-instructions/) | 300–320°F | 15 seconds | High | Cold, after 20–30 seconds | 5–8 seconds under parchment or Teflon |
| [Transfer Kingdom](https://transferkingdom.com/pages/how-to-press-with-a-heatpress) | 300°F | 10–15 seconds | Medium (6–7 on the press's scale) | Cold or warm | 7–10 seconds at 300°F under Teflon or parchment |

A few details from the same pages:

- DTF Transfers Now says it runs cotton at 300–310°F for 10 seconds at 60 PSI on its own automatic press, and that on a clamshell "you should feel real resistance when you close it" ([DTF Transfers Now](https://dtftransfersnow.com/dtf-pressing-instructions)).
- Transfer Kingdom suggests lowering the temperature to 250–280°F and shortening the time by 3–5 seconds for polyester and blends, and keeping zippers, pockets, seams and buttons out of the pressing area ([Transfer Kingdom](https://transferkingdom.com/pages/how-to-press-with-a-heatpress)).
- Transfer Kingdom also notes that "every heat press varies in terms of quality, temperature and pressure", so test on a sample garment before a run ([Transfer Kingdom](https://transferkingdom.com/pages/how-to-press-with-a-heatpress)).

## What that means for the press

### Temperature control

Every maker above presses at 300–320°F or lower, so any press that reaches that range will do. What matters more is that the platen holds the temperature you set. Some makers publish a readout accuracy: Geo Knight lists ±2°F on its Digital Knight presses ([Geo Knight DK20](https://heatpress.com/dk20)).

### Pressure

The makers ask for medium to firm pressure, the same across the design. A household iron can't do that: DTF Transfers Now says irons "can't hold even pressure or consistent heat across the whole design, and DTF needs both" ([DTF Transfers Now](https://dtftransfersnow.com/dtf-pressing-instructions)), and T-shirt.ca says irons aren't recommended for commercial work ([T-shirt.ca](https://t-shirt.ca/dtf-knowledge-hub/heat-press-instructions/)).

### Platen size

Pick a platen at least as big as your largest design, so the whole transfer gets pressed at once. Three of the example presses below use a 16 x 20 inch platen.

### Clamshell, swing-away or draw

- **Clamshell:** the top platen hinges up like a mouth. It needs no side clearance, so it fits a small bench, and it usually costs less. The trade-off is that the hot upper platen sits right above your hands while you lay out the garment ([MyBinding](https://www.mybinding.com/the-differences-between-clamshell-and-swing-away-heat-presses)).
- **Swing-away:** the top platen swings to the side, leaving the work area clear of heat. It needs more room and costs more, and suits thick or large items ([MyBinding](https://www.mybinding.com/the-differences-between-clamshell-and-swing-away-heat-presses)).
- **Draw:** the lower platen slides out toward you. The Hotronix Fusion IQ works as either a swing or a draw press "for a heat-free workspace" ([Stahls'](https://www.stahls.com/fusion-heat-press)).

### Power

A 16 x 20 press draws a lot of current. Geo Knight lists 1800 W at 15 A on 110–120 V for its 16 x 20 presses ([Geo Knight DK20](https://heatpress.com/dk20), [Geo Knight DK20S](https://heatpress.com/dk20s)), and Stahls' says the Fusion IQ needs a designated circuit breaker ([Stahls'](https://www.stahls.com/fusion-heat-press)). Check the outlet you plan to use before you buy.

## Before you buy

- Read the press settings from the maker of the transfers you use most, and check the press reaches and holds them.
- Measure your largest design and pick a platen that covers it.
- Measure the bench: a swing-away needs room to the side.
- Check the circuit: the 16 x 20 presses above list 15 A at 110–120 V.
- Press a test garment before your first paid order.

If you need transfers pressed today and don't own a press yet, the shops listed on this site print DTF locally.
